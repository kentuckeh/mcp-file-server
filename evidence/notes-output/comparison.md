# Software Process Models Comparison

A side-by-side comparative analysis of the Waterfall, Incremental, and Agile software development lifecycle models.

## Comparison Table

| Dimension / Criterion | Waterfall Model | Incremental Model | Agile Model |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Approach & Workflow** | Linear-sequential; phases (requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment) progress strictly one after another. | Modular and successive; system is partitioned into builds delivered incrementally. | Iterative and evolutionary; small cross-functional teams deliver work in short time-boxed sprints. |
| **Requirements Flexibility** | Low / Inflexible; requirements are frozen early. Changes late in the cycle are costly. | Moderate; foundational requirements fixed upfront, but later increments accommodate refinements. | High / Adaptive; requirements evolve continuously based on user feedback and changing priorities. |
| **Delivery of Working Software** | Late in the lifecycle; fully integrated system is delivered at the end of development. | Early and staged; usable subsets of functionality delivered at each successive increment. | Rapid and continuous; working, tested software produced at the end of every sprint (1–4 weeks). |
| **Customer / Stakeholder Involvement** | Concentrated at beginning (specification) and end (acceptance testing / release). | Periodic; users evaluate and provide feedback upon each increment release. | Continuous; active stakeholder collaboration and regular demo reviews throughout. |
| **Risk Management & Testing** | High risk; testing occurs late, making architectural and integration flaws expensive to fix. | Moderate risk; risks managed per increment, though architectural interfaces require careful foresight. | Low risk; continuous automated and exploratory testing with rapid feedback loops. |
| **Project Predictability** | High predictability for fixed-scope, well-understood systems with established deadlines. | Moderate predictability; core roadmap is clear, but final scope/cost may expand. | Lower upfront predictability for total scope/timeline, but high predictability of delivery rhythm and value. |
| **Best Suited For** | Systems with stable, well-defined requirements, critical safety/compliance constraints, and mature tech. | Large systems where core capabilities are needed quickly, but additional features can follow in stages. | Fast-paced environments, startups, dynamic product requirements, and projects requiring rapid customer feedback. |

## Summary
- **Waterfall** emphasizes structure, predictability, and comprehensive upfront documentation.
- **Incremental** balances early value delivery with planned, staged architectural expansion.
- **Agile** prioritizes flexibility, customer collaboration, and iterative refinement.
